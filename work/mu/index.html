01 Overview

In Thailand, “มู” (mu) is shorthand for everyday spiritual practice: wishing, praying and carrying a reminder. Online it is mostly sold as luck. I wanted to design the opposite: a calm moment that asks nothing and gives you something small to keep.

outcomes

02 The problem

Digital mu sells outcomes, not a moment.

Wallpapers, charms and “lucky” posts are popular, but most of them promise results, need an account, or quietly collect data about what people believe. And they leave out anyone whose faith isn't mu.

  • 1

    Promises it can't keep“Wish and it will happen” turns a personal moment into a transaction, and sets people up for disappointment.

  • 2

    Belief is sensitive dataUnder Thailand's PDPA, religious belief is a special category. Most apps treat it like any other click.

  • 3

    One faith fits allPeople who want a quiet moment but don't practise mu get language and rituals that don't fit them.

  • 4

    Too much, too longFeeds and quizzes ask for attention. People just want a short pause and something to carry with them.

04 Key insight

People don't need a promise. They need a moment, and something to keep.

So the product had to be short, kind and honest about what it is: a symbolic activity designed by MU, not a real offering made on your behalf.

No promises

No “guaranteed”, no scores for how many flowers you place, no “strongest deity”.

Private by default

No sign-up. What you type is never sent and is cleared when you leave.

Every faith, its own words

Each path speaks in its own words and ritual, not one script with the names swapped.

Short, then yours

A few minutes, ending in a wallpaper, a share card or a card for your wallet.

06 User flow

Two ways in, one calm path through.

A · Start from what's on your mind

Pick a need (career, study, love, money, comfort) and see the paths that fit it, each with a reason.

B · Start from what you believe

Browse the paths, read where each one comes from, then choose a need. After that the journey is the same.

  1. Choosea need or a path
  2. Prepareoptional context chips
  3. Intendtype it, or keep it silent. Never sent.
  4. Arrangeplace offerings: drag, tap or keyboard
  5. Pausetake your time, no timer
  6. Keepwallpaper · share card · wallet card

07 Wireframes

A home screen that feels like a pause.

The home screen has one job: help someone having a hard day start with a single tap. I sketched four layouts with the same content and compared how quickly each let people begin, and how calm it felt.

  • Chosen · One quiet question. The screen only asks “What's on your mind?” and lists five topics. Nothing else competes for attention.
  • Borrowed · A side menu. Taken from the workspace layout and used on wide screens only. Phones get a floating bottom bar instead.
  • Dropped · Menu grid and dashboard. Both showed everything at once, which felt busy for a moment that should feel calm.

08 Design decisions

Small choices that keep it honest.

Decision 01

Offerings stay at the shrine

The first version put your flowers onto the card you download. But in real practice an offering is left behind, so taking it home reads as taking it back. I split the scene into two panels, the shrine and an offering table, and only the shrine goes onto your card.

ChallengeKeep placing offerings fun without turning the card into a trophy.
SolutionSeparate table, no count or colour score, and an undo for every move.

Decision 02

Your words never leave the page

An intention is personal and can reveal what you believe. The text field lives only in memory. It is cleared when you finish, leave, reload or close the tab, and it is never sent or put into any image you download. You can also skip typing and keep it silent.

ChallengeMake privacy visible, not just a policy page.
SolutionA plain-language note under the field, and a “keep it in my head” button right next to it.

Decision 03

Something real to keep

The ritual ends with something you can keep: a 1080 × 1920 wallpaper, a share card, or a wallet card printed at exactly 85 × 55 mm. The card on screen and the file you download are drawn by the same code, so what you see is what you get.

ChallengeThai text, art and print sizes that look right everywhere.
SolutionOne renderer, fonts loaded before drawing, and failures you can retry without redoing the ritual.

One layout system: a side menu on wide screens, a floating bottom bar on phones.

Key takeaways

  • Fewer choices, easier start. On a hard day, one clear question lowers the effort to begin more than a menu of options.
  • Show privacy in the interface. A short note beside the input and a silent option build more trust than a policy page.
  • Make play safe. Drag, tap and keyboard all work, and every move can be undone, so people explore without fear of mistakes.
  • Design the ending. A keepsake gives a short flow a clear finish and something to take away.

Roadmap

  • Usability testing with each persona, measuring time to start and completion rate.
  • Opt-in, anonymous analytics, already built in, to see which needs and paths people choose.
  • New topics for health and family, based on demand in national surveys.
