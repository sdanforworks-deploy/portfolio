01 — Context

What is CMCC

Capital Market Case Competition (CMCC) is the first and only capital market case competition in Thailand. It gives interested youth an opportunity to unlock their potential and express their creativity, while contributing to the development of Thailand's capital market.

The competition runs each year across multiple rounds, from open application to a final pitch in front of industry judges. Everything around it — registrations, teams, case submissions, scoring — was handled outside a single system.

02 — The brief

One journey, many disconnected steps

The project was assigned to me as an intern, supervised by a Senior UX/UI Designer and scheduled against a timeline set by the team's project manager.

FOR COMPETITORS

A route with no map

Applying, finding teammates, tracking what is due next and where to submit the case all live in different places. Students lose track of the stage they are in.

FOR ORGANISERS

No single source of truth

Applicant data, team composition, submissions and scores need to be read, compared and acted on in one place — including on desktop, during judging.

Design goal

Make the whole competition legible: at any moment, a competitor should know what stage they are in, what is expected of them, and what happens next — and an organiser should be able to see the same picture across every team.

04 — UX flowcharts

Mapping every state before drawing a screen

The flow was documented with a shared notation so engineering, PM and design read the same diagram: pages, sub-pages, windows, page contents, buttons and sub-actions, plus the text, inputs, cards and social entry points attached to each.

NOTATION
  • Page / Sub page / Window
  • Page contents
  • Button / Sub button (action)
  • Text / Text box
  • Input field / Card
  • Action / Social media

06 — UI design

Mobile

The mobile app is where competitors live. The home screen puts the four things they act on — My Team, CMCC Timeline, Tasks and CMCC Socials — one tap from launch, with talent acquisition below.

  • Login & sign up, including OTP verification and password reset
  • Home screen, menu and notifications
  • Team creation and joining an existing team
  • Case upload and task management

07 — UI design

Desktop

Desktop reuses the same components at a wider measure, for the moments that need room: reading a submitted case, filling long forms, and reviewing a team side by side.

  • Same type ramp and colour tokens, re-scaled for desktop
  • Two-column layouts for form plus context
  • Case reading view with the brief kept in sight

08 — UI design

Back office

Two weeks to give organisers the view competitors never see: applicants, teams, submissions and scores in one dashboard, dense by intent.

  • Overview dashboard with counts per competition stage
  • Applicant and team management tables
  • Submission review and final round voting

09 — Testing & feedback

UAT, feedback, final test

Mobile went into user acceptance testing at the end of week ten, before desktop and back office were started — so what testing surfaced could be fixed once, in the system, instead of three times in three UI sets.

01

UAT

02

03

Final test

Re-run after fixes, sign-off before handoff to the build team.

Tester counts and the issue log from UAT sit in the internal project record.

10 — What I took away

Lessons from the first real product

Build the system before the screens

Eight weeks of mobile screens went quickly because type, colour and components were settled first. Desktop and back office then cost three and two weeks instead of starting over.

Flowcharts are a shared language

A notated flow that PM and engineering could read removed most of the questions before they were asked, and made the scope of each screen obvious.

Test before you multiply

Running UAT on mobile before opening desktop meant fixes landed in one place. Testing after all three would have tripled the rework.

Design inside a real timeline

Working to a PM's schedule with a senior reviewer changed how I prioritised: finish what unblocks the next person, document the rest.
